In World War Two, the United States built an army out of balloons.
It's nineteen forty-four. German spies are watching the American front line, and the real troops need to hide.
So the Army creates a secret unit, the Ghost Army. Eleven hundred soldiers, mostly artists, painters, and sound engineers.
Their weapons? Inflatable rubber tanks. Fake trucks. And giant loudspeakers.
A crew rolls out a flat rubber tank, and pumps it full of air in minutes. They stamp fake tracks into the mud.
Then loudspeaker trucks blast the sound of engines and marching boots, all night long.
To the German spies, it looks like thousands of tanks are about to attack.
So while the enemy stares at rubber, the real army slips away, and crosses the river somewhere else.
The Ghost Army pulled off more than twenty of these tricks across Europe.
A handful of artists with air pumps fooled an entire army. Sometimes, the strongest weapon is a paintbrush.
