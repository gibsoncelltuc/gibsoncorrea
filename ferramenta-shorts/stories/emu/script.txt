In nineteen thirty-two, Australia declared war on birds.
Twenty thousand emus were wrecking the wheat farms in Western Australia, and desperate farmers begged the army for help.
So the government sent soldiers, two machine guns, and ten thousand bullets. Against emus.
But emus are fast, and they don't stay put. Whenever the soldiers aimed, the birds scattered in every direction, and sprinted away.
One gunner even mounted a machine gun on a truck. But the truck bounced so much over the rough ground, he couldn't hit a single bird.
After a week, the army had used up thousands of bullets, and the emus were barely scratched.
The soldiers pulled out. The press roared with laughter, and an officer admitted the emus fought like tanks.
The army tried again, and finally claimed a few hundred birds. But the emus kept coming back.
In the end, farmers got a bounty system instead. And the great Emu War went down in history as a defeat for the army.
Humans, zero. Emus, one.
